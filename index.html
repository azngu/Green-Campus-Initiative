Proposal for the Principal and Administration

A cleaner campus makes for clearer minds.

A low-cost, student-led plan to end campus litter, remove daily distractions, and build lasting school pride.

The administration should approve and fund the student-led Green Campus Initiative. It adds waste-sorting stations and a student Clean-up Crew, restoring a clean learning environment and building campus pride.

Why it matters

The University of Salford "Clever Classrooms" study tracked 153 classrooms. Environment shaped results as much as teaching did. Clean, orderly space is the cheapest part of that to fix.

The problem

Overflowing bins and scattered litter pull focus away from learning.

Clutter is a constant distraction

In courtyards, hallways, and homerooms, overflowing bins spill packaging and plastics onto walkways. It looks bad and creates hygiene risks.

Visual disorder is a background distraction. Students who spend breaks in a cluttered courtyard bring lower focus back to class.

With a single bin for everything, food waste mixes with recyclables. The result is odor and pests right next to study areas.

82%

of surveyed students say visible litter hurts their mood and concentration.

70%

of loose litter was logged within 15 feet of an already overfilled, unsorted bin.

Our plan

Three actions students will carry out themselves.

Waste-sorting stations

Replace single bins in busy areas with color-coded stations for recycling, compost, and landfill, with clear picture signs.

Goal: 40% less bin overflow.

15-minute Clean-up Crew

A rotating student team sweeps key courtyards and hallways during Friday homeroom, so the campus is clean before the weekend.

Goal: no lost class time.

Peer culture shift

Student-designed posters and homeroom announcements turn cleanliness from a chore into something to be proud of.

Goal: lasting student ownership.

Concerns, answered

Questions the administration is likely to ask.

Isn't cleaning the job of custodial staff?

Custodians handle deep cleaning, sanitation, and repairs. Daily student litter pulls them away from that work. With sorting bins and a student crew, students prevent the mess instead of leaving it behind, and custodians can focus on facility upkeep.

Will students keep it up all year?

Clean-up slots sit inside existing Friday homeroom time, 15 minutes at a time. Clubs and honor societies earn volunteer service hours, so leadership rotates and interest stays high.

Does this cost the school much?

No. The whole plan needs a one-time budget of $150 for three sorting stations, signs, and basic cleanup gear.
